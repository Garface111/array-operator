Vermont net-metering rate — at a glance

Set by the Public Utility Commission's biennial update and published in each utility's tariff. Array Operator always invoices from the rate on the offtaker's actual settled bill — the figures below are the reference the state sets.

WhatRateNotes
Statewide blended residential rate $0.1839/kWh The base credit value the PUC blends from utility residential rates.
Category I effective rate (net-metered ≤ 15 kW) ≈ $0.1439/kWh ↓ Aug 1, 2026 Blended rate minus the siting/REC adjustor, which the PUC raised 4¢ → 5¢/kWh for permits Aug 1, 2026 – Jul 31, 2028 (down ~2¢ from $0.1639).
Small-system bonus (< 15 kW) +$0.01/kWh Added for the first 10 years; +$0.03/kWh more if RECs are transferred to the utility.
Rate lock ~10 years An array's rate is fixed for its net-metering term (from the permit/CPG date), then rolls to the blended rate in effect at renewal.

This is the 7th consecutive year the PUC has cut the Category I adjustor.

Reference only — always confirm the current figure against the utility's filed tariff or the offtaker's bill before relying on it. Array Operator's invoices already read the bill's own rate.

Active rate cases & regulatory status

2026 Biennial Net-Metering Update — PUC Case 26-0291-INV

Order issued May 29, 2026 · effective Aug 1, 2026. The Commission's statutorily required update to net-metering compensation. It raised the small-solar siting charge from 4¢ to 5¢/kWh (a further cut to Category I). The Department of Public Service urged the Commission to instead open a broader rulemaking to reform net metering — worth watching, as it could reshape compensation beyond the biennial formula.

Utilities updated their tariffs by June 15, 2026; new rates apply to permits filed on or after Aug 1, 2026.

Group / community ("offsite") net metering is being phased out

H.289 (enacted June 2024) overhauled Vermont's Renewable Energy Standard and phased out offsite / "virtual" group net metering starting January 2025, with a one-year extension for systems serving multifamily or low-income housing. A successor-program analysis was mandated but no replacement is yet in place.

What it means for operators: existing group-net-metering arrays keep operating under their agreements — your current fleet is unaffected — but Vermont is not approving new community-solar offtaker groups today. If you're planning expansion, this is the file to watch.

Renewable Energy Standard (RES) — the demand side of your RECs

Under H.289, each utility's renewable requirement rises from 63% (2025) to 100% by 2030 (2035 for GF Power and certain municipals). Tier II (distributed ≤ 5 MW) starts at 5.8%; Tier III (energy transformation) rises 7.33% → 12% by 2032. The update also lets every provider except GMP access RECs from pre-2017 net-metering projects — a shift in who can claim the environmental attributes your arrays produce.
